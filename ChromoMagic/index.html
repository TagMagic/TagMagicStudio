The History of Color Light

Ancient roots. Healers in ancient Egypt, Greece, India and China all linked colors with the body and the spirit. India's Ayurvedic tradition paired colors with the chakras, the energy centers from the base of the spine to the crown of the head.

Avicenna (around 1025 AD). The Persian physician wrote about color in his famous Canon of Medicine, treating color both as a sign of illness and as something that could affect the body.

Edwin Babbitt (1878). His book The Principles of Light and Color started the modern movement, sorting colors into warming, stimulating "red" rays and calming, cooling "blue" rays.

Dinshah Ghadiali (1920s). His Spectro-Chrome system used a lamp with twelve colored glass slides, sold across America in the art deco years. His medical claims were challenged in court, but his color system is still followed by color therapists today.

Today. Science doesn't support color light as a cure for illness. It does show that light color affects alertness and sleep, and many people find colored light calming and beautiful. ChromoMagic is made for relaxing and enjoying color.

About the Tones

Chakra tones use the popular Solfeggio frequencies matched to the chakra colors (396 Hz for red up to 963 Hz for violet). Turquoise and magenta use the extended Solfeggio tones 285 Hz and 174 Hz.

Light octave tones take the real frequency of each color of light, hundreds of trillions of vibrations per second, and halve it 40 times until it lands in the range you can hear. The tone you hear is the color's own vibration, 40 octaves down.

Magenta is special: it isn't in the rainbow at all. Your brain creates it by mixing red and violet light, so its light octave tone plays red and violet together.

Tip: the radio's knobs and switches work too. The knobs change the color, the left switch picks the tone style, and the right switch turns the tone on and off.
